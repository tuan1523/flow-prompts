Close-up lifestyle photo of an EMBROIDERED baby tee: the attached design is sewn onto the center chest as thick, raised, glossy MACHINE EMBROIDERY — the kind of premium embroidered boutique tee where the stitched design is obviously made of thread even from arm's length.

PRIORITY ORDER (when rules compete, the higher one wins):
1. The design reads unmistakably as real stitched thread at first glance.
2. Every word, letter and number is spelled exactly as in the attachment, in the same layout, arrangement and colors.
3. The garment is a fitted cropped baby tee.
4. Scene, pose and styling.
Line thickness and surface finish are allowed to change to make rule 1 true; wording, layout and composition are not.

THE ATTACHMENT IS ONLY A STITCH MAP: use it to know WHAT the design shows — its words, drawing, layout and colors. Ignore everything else in it: any garment, model, pose, props, lighting and the flat ink surface of the artwork. Never transfer its pixels onto the tee; rebuild every part of the design from thread.

EMBROIDERY — HOW THE DESIGN IS BUILT:
- Headline lettering = satin-stitch columns 3-4 mm wide, tightly packed diagonal thread passes, domed and glossy, standing 1.5-2 mm proud of the fabric. The diagonal thread rows are visible inside every letter.
- Small lettering = narrower satin columns about 2 mm wide, still clearly raised and shiny, each letter slightly bolder than the attached font but in the same style.
- Drawings, sketch lines and outlines = digitized for embroidery: every thin line thickened to a satin column at least 1.5 mm wide, hatching simplified into fewer, bolder stitched lines, the drawing still instantly recognizable with the same silhouette and composition.
- Any filled color area = dense tatami fill, the STITCH ANGLE ROTATED differently in adjacent blocks so each block catches light differently.
- Strong directional sheen: rows facing the light glow bright, neighboring rows turn darker purely from thread direction — the design shimmers in highlight and shadow bands that flat ink never has.
- A visible seam where two thread colors meet; tiny fringe of thread tips and needle penetration points along outer edges.
- Embroidery style: {flat satin machine embroidery throughout|Thêu phẳng satin / 3D puff embroidery on the largest headline lettering — thick foam-raised, rounded pillowy letters — with flat satin embroidery on everything else|Chữ lớn thêu nổi 3D puff}

RELIEF CUES THAT MUST SURVIVE AT PHOTO DISTANCE (these, not microscopic detail, make it read as embroidery):
- A soft contact shadow hugging every stitched letter and line on the side away from the light.
- The jersey visibly pulled by the stitching: small puckers and fine radial wrinkles a few millimeters around the lettering and denser areas, tiny dimples at needle holes — while the design itself holds its shape as a firm stitched patch and stays undistorted.
- The fabric texture runs UNDER the design and is interrupted by it: the knit's ribs or grain stop at the thread edge, clearly showing the thread sits ON TOP of the fabric.
- The stitched area has a different material look from the matte tee: glossy, ridged, slightly stiffer.

FRAMING: shot about 40-50 cm away, vertical 3:4. The frame runs from the chin (a hint of lips at most, no eyes) down to the bare midriff just under the hem; the shoulders are cropped by the frame edges. The embroidery spans at least 40% of the frame width and sits in sharp focus, the sharpest area of the whole image; the background softly blurred.

LIGHT: bright natural daylight with a strong LOW RAKING side light — clean white sunlight skimming across the chest from one side at a shallow angle, centered on the design, so every satin ridge gleams on one side and casts a crisp micro-shadow on the other, and the puckers around the stitching show as soft highlight-and-shadow ripples. The rest of the scene stays bright and airy. Neutral white balance, whites stay pure white. On white, cream, light pink and baby blue tees, deepen the contact shadows so the stitched relief stands out against the pale fabric.

PHOTO STYLE: candid Pinterest / Etsy apparel photo, slightly imperfect framing, true-to-life colors, very subtle grain — but the embroidery itself stays crisp and detailed.

FINAL CHECK: look at the design as a whole — if any letter, line or area could pass for ink on fabric, rebuild it with visible raised satin thread, sheen bands and a contact shadow.

AVOID: flat ink-on-fabric surface, sticker or heat-transfer look, hairline strokes, smooth vector edges, misspelled or distorted lettering, extra text or logos, visible face or eyes, distorted hands, boxy or oversized shirt, embroidery on sleeves or hem, anything covering the design, watermarks.
